# Content Guide

## Purpose
To give the client (and anyone writing copy for the site) clear rules for tone, formatting, and structure, so content stays consistent with the brand regardless of who writes it.

## Overview
Moments in Blooms' brand voice is warm, refined, and confident — descriptive without being overwrought, premium without being cold. This guide translates that into practical writing rules for each editable content area.

## Current Status
Drafted during project scoping; to be shared with the client ahead of content entry for Milestone 2/4.

## Responsibilities

**General Writing Rules**
- Write in second person ("you") when addressing prospective clients directly (e.g., Services, Contact), and warm third-person/first-person-plural ("we") when describing the business (e.g., About).
- Prefer specific, sensory language over generic marketing adjectives — "hand-tied garden roses in blush and ivory" rather than "beautiful floral arrangements."
- Keep sentences relatively short; this is a visually-led site, and copy should support the imagery, not compete with it.
- Avoid exclamation points and overtly salesy language ("Book now!!", "Don't miss out!") — the brand voice is confident, not urgent or pushy.

**Homepage**
- Hero heading: short, evocative, brand-defining (one line, ideally under ~8 words).
- Subheading: one sentence expanding on the hero, oriented toward what the visitor can expect from the business.

**About**
- Written in first-person-plural ("we"), covering the business's story, philosophy, and what sets its styling approach apart. Avoid resume-style history; focus on approach and values.

**Services**
- Each service needs: a clear title, a 2–4 sentence description focused on the experience/outcome (not just a feature list), and a representative image.
- Avoid pricing in service descriptions unless specifically requested by the client — pricing/quoting is out of current scope (`PROJECT_SCOPE.md`) and handled off-platform for now.

**Gallery**
- Captions should be short (under ~12 words) and specific — event type and a standout detail (e.g., "Garden wedding, Yarra Valley — ivory and sage palette") rather than generic ("Beautiful wedding setup").
- Every gallery image requires a caption, both for brand consistency and for accessibility `alt` text (see `SEO.md`).

**FAQs**
- Questions phrased as the client's actual prospective customers would ask them, in plain language ("How far in advance should I book styling?") rather than formal/legal phrasing.
- Answers should be direct and complete but concise — a paragraph, not a page.

**Contact**
- Keep the contact page copy minimal — its job is to reduce friction to enquiry, not to re-sell the business (that's what Home/About/Services are for).

## Implementation Notes
This guide should be handed to the client (or any copywriter they engage) before content entry begins, so the developer isn't in the position of editorializing client copy during CMS training.
